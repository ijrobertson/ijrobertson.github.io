Por vs Para in Spanish

📚 Explore Spanish: Spanish Basics First Conversation At the Restaurant At the Hotel At the Airport Typical Day Spanish Hub →
Spanish grammar guide · Updated October 2026 · 10 min read

The short answer

Para points forward to a goal: a purpose, a destination, a recipient or a deadline (Este regalo es para ti — This gift is for you). Por points back to a cause, or describes how something happens: the reason, route, duration, exchange or means (Gracias por el regalo — Thanks for the gift). If you can say “in order to” or “headed for”, use para. If you can say “because of”, “through”, “in exchange for” or “per”, use por.

Por and Para at a Glance

PARA purpose destination recipient deadline employer opinion comparison
POR cause duration through / along exchange means on behalf of per by (passive)

Por and para both usually translate as “for” in English, which is exactly why they cause so much confusion. Spanish splits the job of “for” in two, and choosing the wrong one can change the meaning of your sentence. The good news: once you understand the idea behind each word, most choices become automatic. This guide walks through every use with real example sentences, shows you pairs where the choice changes the meaning, and finishes with a quiz so you can test yourself.

The Trick to Remember Por vs Para

Picture para as an arrow → pointing at an end point. That end point can be a goal (para aprender), a place (para Madrid), a person (para ti) or a moment in time (para el lunes).

Picture por as looking at what is behind or around the action: the reason it happened (por la lluvia), the path it took (por el parque), how long it lasted (por dos horas) or what was traded for it (por diez euros).

When you are unsure, try the English test:

When to Use Para

Para looks ahead. It is used for goals, destinations and the people or deadlines something is aimed at.

PARA

1. Purpose or goal

“in order to” + infinitive, or what something is for
  • Estudio español para viajar a México.I study Spanish (in order) to travel to Mexico.
  • Ahorro dinero para comprar un coche.I’m saving money to buy a car.
  • Hago ejercicio para estar sano.I exercise to stay healthy.
  • Es una taza para café.It’s a coffee cup (a cup for coffee).
  • ¿Para qué sirve esto?What is this for?
💡 Tip: English often drops “in order” and just says “to”. Spanish never drops the para: Vine para ayudarte (I came to help you), not Vine ayudarte.
PARA

2. Destination

where someone or something is headed
  • Mañana salgo para Madrid.Tomorrow I leave for Madrid.
  • El tren para Sevilla sale a las ocho.The train to Seville leaves at eight.
  • Vamos para la playa.We’re heading to the beach.
PARA

3. Recipient

who something is intended for
  • Este regalo es para ti.This gift is for you.
  • Hice la cena para mis padres.I made dinner for my parents.
  • Hay una carta para usted.There’s a letter for you.
PARA

4. Deadline

“by” or “due” a certain time
  • Necesito el informe para el viernes.I need the report by Friday.
  • La tarea es para mañana.The homework is due tomorrow.
  • Para las diez ya estaré en casa.By ten o’clock I’ll already be home.
PARA

5. Employer

who you work for
  • Trabajo para una empresa alemana.I work for a German company.
  • Mi hermana trabaja para el gobierno.My sister works for the government.
PARA

6. Opinion

“for me” / “in my opinion”
  • Para mí, el español es más fácil que el francés.For me, Spanish is easier than French.
  • Para ella, la familia es lo más importante.For her, family is the most important thing.
PARA

7. Comparison with what’s expected

“for a…” / “considering…”
  • Para ser niño, habla muy bien.For a child, he speaks very well.
  • Hace mucho calor para ser octubre.It’s very hot for October.

When to Use Por

Por looks at the reason behind an action or at how it happens: the route, the time span, the price paid or the way it was done.

POR

1. Cause or reason

“because of”, “due to”, “for the sake of”
  • Gracias por tu ayuda.Thanks for your help.
  • Llegué tarde por el tráfico.I arrived late because of the traffic.
  • Lo hago por mis hijos.I do it for (the sake of) my kids.
  • Lo castigaron por mentir.They punished him for lying.
POR

2. Duration

how long something lasts
  • Viví en Lima por dos años.I lived in Lima for two years.
  • Estudié por tres horas.I studied for three hours.
💬 How natives say it: por with a length of time is correct, but native speakers very often use durante or no preposition at all: Viví dos años en Lima. Never use para here.
POR

3. Movement through, along or around

the route, or a general area
  • Caminamos por el parque.We walked through the park.
  • Pasamos por Barcelona.We passed through Barcelona.
  • El ladrón entró por la ventana.The thief came in through the window.
  • ¿Hay un banco por aquí?Is there a bank around here?
POR

4. Exchange or price

“in exchange for”
  • Pagué veinte euros por la camisa.I paid twenty euros for the shirt.
  • Te cambio mi bocadillo por tu manzana.I’ll trade you my sandwich for your apple.
  • Lo vendí por muy poco.I sold it for very little.
POR

5. Means of communication

how a message gets somewhere
  • Hablamos por teléfono.We talked on the phone.
  • Te lo mando por correo electrónico.I’ll send it to you by email.
  • Lo vi por la tele.I saw it on TV.
POR

6. On behalf of / instead of

doing something in someone’s place
  • ¿Puedes trabajar por mí el sábado?Can you work for me (cover my shift) on Saturday?
  • Firmé por mi jefe.I signed on behalf of my boss.
POR

7. Rates: per, by, times

speed, frequency, percentages, multiplication
  • Iba a cien kilómetros por hora.He was going a hundred kilometers per hour.
  • Voy al gimnasio dos veces por semana.I go to the gym twice a week.
  • El diez por ciento de los alumnos.Ten percent of the students.
  • Tres por cuatro son doce.Three times four is twelve.
POR

8. The passive “by”

who did the action
  • Don Quijote fue escrito por Cervantes.Don Quixote was written by Cervantes.
  • La casa fue construida por mi abuelo.The house was built by my grandfather.
POR

9. Parts of the day

general time, without a specific hour
  • Estudio por la mañana.I study in the morning.
  • Nos vemos por la tarde.See you in the afternoon.
  • No me gusta conducir por la noche.I don’t like driving at night.
🌎 Regional note: in much of Latin America you’ll also hear en la mañana. With a specific hour, use de: a las ocho de la mañana.
POR

10. Going to get something

after verbs of motion like ir, venir, pasar
  • Voy por pan.I’m going to get bread.
  • Paso por ti a las siete.I’ll pick you up at seven.

Por vs Para Side by Side

Many uses come in matching pairs. Para marks the end point; por marks the cause or the path.

IdeaParaPor
Why?Purpose (goal ahead)Estudio para aprobar.Cause (reason behind)Aprobé por estudiar.
Where?DestinationVoy para Lima.Route or areaPaso por Lima.
When?DeadlinePara el lunes.Duration or part of dayPor dos semanas.
Who?Recipient or employerEs para Ana.On behalf of, or agentLo hice por Ana.

Same Sentence, Different Meaning

Sometimes both words are grammatically correct, but they say different things. These pairs are the best way to feel the difference.

Para
Lo hice para ti.I made it for you. (you receive it)
Por
Lo hice por ti.I did it because of you / for your sake.
Para
Trabajo para mi padre.I work for my father. (he’s my boss)
Por
Trabajo por mi padre.I’m working in my father’s place.
Para
Compré flores para María.I bought flowers for María. (to give her)
Por
Compré flores por María.I bought flowers on María’s behalf / because of her.
Para
Caminamos para el centro.We walked toward downtown.
Por
Caminamos por el centro.We walked around downtown.
Para
¿Para qué estudias español?What are you studying Spanish for? (the goal)
Por
¿Por qué estudias español?Why do you study Spanish? (the reason)

Common Expressions with Por and Para

These fixed phrases don’t always follow the rules neatly, so it’s easiest to learn them as vocabulary.

With por

por favor — please
por fin — finally, at last
por supuesto — of course
por eso — that’s why
por ejemplo — for example
por lo menos — at least
por cierto — by the way
por si acaso — just in case
por lo general — generally
por ahora — for now
por casualidad — by chance
por lo visto — apparently

With para

para siempre — forever
para nada — not at all
para que + subjunctive — so that
para colmo — to top it all off
no es para tanto — it’s not a big deal
para variar — for a change
para entonces — by then
¿para qué? — what for?

Common Mistakes English Speakers Make

Gracias para tu ayuda.Gracias por tu ayuda.“Thank you” always takes por: you’re thankful because of something.
Pagué cincuenta dólares para los zapatos.Pagué cincuenta dólares por los zapatos.Money in exchange for something is por.
Estudio por aprender.Estudio para aprender.“In order to” + infinitive is always para.
Fue escrito para Cervantes.Fue escrito por Cervantes.The person who did a passive action is introduced with por.
Trabajo para la mañana.Trabajo por la mañana.Parts of the day take por (para la mañana means “by tomorrow morning”).
Busco para mis llaves.Busco mis llaves.Some verbs already include “for”: buscar (look for), esperar (wait for), pedir (ask for). No preposition needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between por and para?

Para points forward to a goal: a purpose, destination, recipient or deadline (Este regalo es para ti). Por points back to a cause or describes how something happens: the reason, route, duration, exchange or means (Gracias por el regalo). If you can say “in order to” or “headed for”, use para. If you can say “because of”, “through”, “in exchange for” or “per”, use por.

Is it “gracias por” or “gracias para”?

Always gracias por. You are thanking someone because of something they did or in exchange for it, which are both uses of por: Gracias por tu ayuda (Thanks for your help).

What is the difference between ¿por qué? and ¿para qué?

¿Por qué? asks for the cause (why did this happen?), and ¿para qué? asks for the purpose (what is this for?). ¿Por qué estudias español? expects an answer like porque me gusta; ¿Para qué estudias español? expects an answer like para trabajar en México.

Is there an easy trick to remember por vs para?

Think of para as an arrow pointing at an end point: a goal, a place, a person or a deadline. Think of por as describing the reason behind something or the path it takes. Then test with English: “in order to” or “by (a deadline)” means para; “because of”, “through”, “per” or “in exchange for” means por.

Can por and para both be correct in the same sentence?

Yes, but the meaning changes. Lo hice para ti means I made it for you (you receive it), while Lo hice por ti means I did it because of you or for your sake. Trabajo para mi padre means he is your employer; Trabajo por mi padre means you are working in his place. See more pairs above.

Do I use por or para with time?

Use para for a deadline (La tarea es para el lunes, The homework is due Monday). Use por for a length of time (Estudié por dos horas) and for general parts of the day (por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche).
